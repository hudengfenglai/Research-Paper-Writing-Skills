# Introduction writing guide

The Introduction should lead the reader from a meaningful problem to a precise, evidence-bound contribution.

## Recommended progression

1. **Task and setting.** Define the input, output, and application or scientific value.
2. **Closest prior approaches.** Group them by the mechanism or assumption relevant to the paper.
3. **Unresolved gap.** State the observed limitation and the technical reason it occurs. Do not invent a gap from a generic weakness.
4. **Our response.** Explain the actual insight, mechanism, and expected consequence. Point to the overview figure when useful.
5. **Evidence preview.** Summarize the experiments that test the central claims.
6. **Contributions.** Give two to four precise, falsifiable bullets.

## Paragraph test

The first sentence states the paragraph's job. The remaining sentences provide mechanism, evidence, or a transition to the next job. If a paragraph cannot be reverse-outlined into one topic sentence and supporting points, split or remove it.

## Contribution bullet template

- We identify or formalize `[problem/gap]` under `[setting]`.
- We propose `[method or insight]`, which addresses `[technical reason]` through `[mechanism]`.
- We show `[verified result]` under `[protocol]`, with `[scope or limitation]`.

Do not use contribution bullets to relabel routine implementation steps as scientific novelty.
